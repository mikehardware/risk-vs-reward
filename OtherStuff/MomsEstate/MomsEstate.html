Mom's Estate

My brother and I had a dispute over our mother’s estate. I want to provide a clear, condensed version of the events, supported by the documentation, so that anyone interested can review the record and make up their own mind. This is only a summary; there was considerably more correspondence between the attorneys than I have included here - the stack of letters is at least an inch thick. Please ask if you would like to see any additional materials.

1990

Mom’s will was straightforward: her debts were to be paid, my brother and an uncle were named as co-executors, and everything remaining was to be divided equally between my brother and me.

1990-06-26-Mom-Will-from-Probate_Redacted.pdf

The executors had 60 days to provide an accounting of the estate. (See page 2. So until the end of August 1990.)

1990-06-26-Court_Redacted.pdf

It ultimately took until July 1993, and court proceedings to require the executor to complete the process before the estate was settled. The final judgment, including the accounts and amounts, puts the claims, account balances, and outcome into perspective.

1993-10-14-Court-Order_Redacted.pdf

A little history provides some context. I had always been close to my family. My brother and I visited and called often during the years before these problems arose. In 1989, I took our mother, my daughter, and Fred’s daughters to a family reunion in Kentucky. Although I expressed concerns to him about his sudden remarriage after his divorce, I was his best man in December 1990. My family also enjoyed our visit with mom over Christmas, shortly before she passed away.

In a letter dated December 7, 1992, Fred offered another explanation, now claiming that he and I had a verbal agreement.

Such an agreement did not exist. At that point, the explanations I had received had changed substantially over time: the initial position was that there was no problem; later, there were claims concerning money supposedly mishandled by me; then there was the claim that our mother had gifted Fred $10,000 from the CD, or provided me with $15,000 (or later $8,000) for a house down payment; and, as the matter approached court, there was now a claim of a verbal agreement.

My position throughout was that the will specified an equal division of the estate, and that the estate funds I handled were used to pay estate expenses and were documented. I was seeking an accounting and the distribution of my share of the estate, as required by law.

The estate accounting had still not been filed by the agreed-upon November date. On December 9, 1992, my attorney filed a “Motion to Hold Executors in Contempt and to Remove”.

1993

The court heard the matter on January 5, 1993, and “determined the executors were required to file an accounting regardless of whether they continued serving and that such accounting was overdue.” The court set January 29 as the deadline, with contempt proceedings to follow if the accounting was not filed.

Fred finally filed an estate accounting with the court. I was then able to file my objections.

In July, the case came before the judge for review of the accounts and discrepancies. To save legal expenses, I represented myself. The judge went through my list of accounts and discrepancies. I was able to show the checks Fred received and what he’d sent – which did not always represent an equal division. Things went quickly and efficiently.

1993-10-14-Court-Order_Redacted.pdf

The judge ultimately ruled that each of us should pay our own attorney fees. I was prepared to then ask for damages, but at that point I had a very unusual experience – like one that had happened eleven years earlier.

In 1982, on my first job out of college, I was living in Maryland and had been a Christian for only about two years. We were just starting out, and my living expenses were unexpectedly high. Things were difficult, and I was praying about the situation at work. I happened to be sitting in the men’s room when I heard someone to my right say, “Don’t worry about it.”

I looked under the partition and saw that nobody was there. Then I heard it again. I also experienced what felt like a hand beginning at the back of my head and moving down my spine, taking the tension and fear away. I was stunned. Either I had just lost my mind, or something very real and important had just occurred. If I’d lost my mind, the problems would continue. I decided I’d better not worry about the finances, and see what happened.

Things began improving rapidly. An unexpected refund arrived. The car loan was refinanced at a much better rate. Other things, both large and small, worked out unexpectedly. Whatever someone might call that experience, it was very real.

Then, in that courtroom in Centerville in 1993, as I was preparing to ask the judge for damages, I heard a voice to my right say, “No.”

I turned my head, but nobody was there. Then I heard it again and experienced that same hand down my spine taking the tension away.

I thought, “OK, Lord.”

The judge asked if I had anything else, and I said no.

That left me with about $7,000 in attorney fees. A few days later, we received unexpected good news: a windfall of $7,000 from another source.

For me, the timing was remarkable. After everything that had happened, I took it as another reminder that God was there and that I did not need to carry the burden any further.

The estate dispute had finally come to an end.
