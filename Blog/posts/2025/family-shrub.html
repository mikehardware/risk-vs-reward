The Family Shrub

Posted July 26, 2025

If you're thinking of delving into your family genealogy, be prepared and brace yourself for whatever you may find. Here's some of the things I've discovered.

Let's start with the obvious. Every person on Earth is related, if you go back far enough. For some folks, you don't have to go back terribly far. Like me and my wife - she's my 10th cousin because we had a common ancestor who was born in 1580. (I would have never known we were already related if I hadn't started this hobby. It's OK, at that level, genetically we're basically unrelated.)

Then there are people with… unusual names. Humor writer Dave Barry, a favorite of mine, has a website that posts funny tidbits, including a strict policy of not making fun of people's names (by posting them). There's a stereotype about folks in the South marrying their cousins. Well, I found a case where first cousins married. They had a child, and that child also married their first cousin. That part of the family tree looks more like a family shrub.

Have you ever heard the old song, “I'm My Own Grandpa”? I found something almost as strange. A father had a son, and his wife died. He and his son then married a set of sisters, and each had children. My head spins trying to figure out the relationships. Heres what AI tells me:

Father's relation to Son's wife - Daughter-in-law/Sister-in-law, Son's relation to Father's wife - Stepmother/Sister-in-law, Father's wife to Son - Stepson/Brother-in-law, Son's wife to Son's Father - Father-in-law/Brother-in-law, Son's children to Son's Father - Grandchild/Niece or Nephew, Father's new children to Son - Half-sibling/Niece or Nephew-in law, New children of both Father and Son to each other - double cousins

The census information can also be fascinating. I found out the value of houses way back then was only about $300. (Guess you can save a lot without all the modern frills, like electricity, air conditioning, indoor plumbing, inflation, …)

Then in a much more serious note, be prepared for what you are likely to find, as in unusual causes of death. Some I've seen include war (like the USS Indianapolis as mentioned in the movie Jaws), pellagra, being dragged by horse, accidental poisonings, mine collapse, the Jonestown massacre, and even murder. One of those was fascinating, and really ought to be made into a movie. It involves racial injustice, lynching, and future Supreme Court justice Thurgood Marshall and FBI director J. Edgar Hoover. I'll give that its own future blog post.
