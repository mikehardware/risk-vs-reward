Memories of my Brother

Posted August 15, 2025

My brother passed away recently, so I'd like to share some of my favorite memories of him.

Fred was born about 16 months before me, and with the timing of things, was in the grade ahead of me as we grew up. He was a classic older brother to me. I looked up to him, after all he was bigger and could do things I couldn't. We'd tussle like all brothers, but he also looked out for me.

Fred was outgoing, with lots of friends, while I was more introverted (due to my prosopagnosia) but I was always glad to be included in whatever he was doing.

Our home in Nashville had a big den area, and when Hot Wheels first came out, we spent hours racing them. We built elaborate track layouts and came up with new tricks and challenges. Life then felt like a big adventure. We could ride our bikes all over the neighborhood, play in the woods, join in games with the other kids, shoot our BB guns, and spend endless summer hours swimming in our little backyard pool - or when we travelled, get sunburnt while spending most of the day in a real motel pool.

In the summer of 1969, our family took an epic road trip - from Tennessee across the country to Oregon and back. We visited relatives along the way and camped in the car some nights at national parks.

When our dad retired, we moved to rural Hickman County, Tennessee, which opened up a whole new chapter of adventure for our high school years. Our family literally built our own home there! Dad offered us a whole $5 each to dig the hole for the septic tank by hand. Fred helped lay the cement block walls while I mixed mortar. He also worked on the electrical wiring. We learned real lessons about independence and self-reliance - and how to deal with chickens, pigs, and cows.

Fred was also handy with cars. I remember how he installed a cool 8-track player in the old 1962 Rambler station wagon that I drove to college. That meant a lot to me and kept me entertained and alert for all those miles.

The memories of those years remain vivid and meaningful to me. They remind me of who Fred was at his best - curious, capable, full of energy, and ready for the next adventure. I'm grateful for those times, and I treasure his memory. Life took us down different paths later on, but I choose to remember the good, and I hold onto those early years with love.
